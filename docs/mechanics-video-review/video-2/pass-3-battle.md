No legible player/target HP ledger or stamina meter was established. A moving stick graphic without visible touch/input telemetry does not prove it causes actor motion.
- Several damage numbers are present before the first observed strike, remain during recovery/approach, change while no separate new contact is clear, and eventually stack. Numeric labels are therefore **visual evidence only**. Do not copy −145, −203, 678, 789, 1024 or other values into Ashen balance or infer a critical percentage.
- Frequent large chest spark fans partly hide the weapon/body intersection. Fire covers the enemy and floor; apparent overlap cannot establish physically correct contact, burn damage or heat hazards. There is no visible blocked/missed/dodged counterfactual with a trusted health change.
- The enemy's silhouette/material details and late fiery blade-like hand shape evolve; HUD details/number persistence and repeated choreography are consistent with illustrative or generated game-like footage. Production method is **not proven by pixels**. No underlying engine, actual playability, physics solver, rigid-body simulation or authored state machine can be claimed.
- The whole clip supports a one-enemy confrontation. Radar dots are not evidence of off-screen AI participation. No projectile flight, multi-target cleave, progression, resource use, loot, death or victory is demonstrated.

## Proposed Ashen translation (separate from source facts)

1. **Distinct boss attack families and telegraphs.** Consider a clearly prepared claw sweep and an overhead ground attack, each with a committed direction, visible active interval and recovery. V2-B04/B06/B09/B12 motivate their visual distinction; they do not prescribe exact timing. The current seam is `app/game/engine.ts:393` (enemy strike construction) plus `roster-assets.ts:64` (phase mapping). Explicit action IDs/attack definitions should replace accidental dependence on one universal four-strike cycle if the design selects these patterns. Validate no early damage, one accepted hit per target per action, interrupt policy and reach at actual AI approach distance.
2. **An optional warning/eruption hazard.** V2-B03 suggests a flat warning followed by a vertical eruption. If approved, define warning time, footprint, lock/track policy, active lifetime, damage cadence, dodge interaction and cleanup independently of VFX. Give the warning higher contrast than decorative lava. Test entry/exit boundaries, hitches, pause, death/reset, hidden effects, and low-quality rendering so hazard rules never depend on particles being drawn. Ashen's current immediate Q AoE is not an existing general hazard system.
3. **Readable evasive response.** V2-B09/B13 support a low evasive posture alongside an enemy sweep. Decide whether Ashen should use its existing dodge with improved animation or a new defence mechanic; do not infer duck-specific hurtboxes or parry from the footage. Test committed/directional movement, start/end immunity, cancellation priority, wall collision, keyboard/touch parity and visible recovery.
4. **Accepted-contact feedback with bounded labels.** Use a short local burst, directional enemy reaction and optional damage text on actual accepted hits; suppress it for rejected/shielded contacts. Existing seams: `engine.ts:241`, `:321`, `:339`, `combat-animation.ts` and combat feedback. Limit particle/label count, lifetime and overlap; preserve reduced-motion and mobile budgets. Avoid source-style full-chest spark blankets or persistent text hiding a windup.
5. **Critical hits require a new explicit rule if chosen.** The footage has critical labels, but supplies no causal mechanism. Define eligibility, multiplier, randomness/seed, feedback and tests independently; otherwise retain ordinary damage and borrow only emphasis for a selected heavy hit. Maintain single-hit bookkeeping, truthful health arithmetic and death/reward deduplication. This clip supplies no result-state requirement.

The strongest additional reference beyond Video 1 is the visual distinction between **melee preparation, evasive posture, and a floor patch that becomes an eruption**. The source does not establish their simulation rules. Keep proposed tuning and architecture decisions labelled as decisions in the handoff, with testable behavior, rather than claiming those rules were recovered from footage.
