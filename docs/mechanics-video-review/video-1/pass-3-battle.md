## UI evidence and authenticity limits

1. From f1 the screen has `Back`, a camera icon, **two buttons both labelled `Attack`**, `Defend`, `Magic`, `Item`, and **two circular map-like displays**. An Item button is initially red; the right Attack button is highlighted red later. No finger, cursor or discrete touch indicator is shown. Button colour cannot prove an input was pressed or held, and duplicated controls should not be copied into Ashen as a requirements list.
2. `HP: 100/100` remains visible through close exchanges until the HUD disappears. The green bar shows small visual variation. This does not prove invulnerability, perfect blocking, misses, or a no-damage rule; the overlay itself is inconsistent.
3. Target labels morph and mix numbers with percentage notation (`755/1000` → `50%/1000` → `755/1000`; later `40%/1000`/`43%/1000`). Their numerical text, bar length and damage labels are not a consistent simulation trace. Expanded frames 33, 89, 97, 145 and 156 preserve direct evidence.
4. At the last attack the upper map partially fades before the rest of the UI vanishes; victory/critical typography scales dramatically and then settles. The scene also continually reframes the two combatants. Treat this as a presentation reference rather than proof of a player-controlled combat camera or input lock.
5. The changing/malformed overlays and highly choreographed repeated exchanges are **consistent with generated or illustrative game-like footage**, but pixels alone do not establish its production method. The actionable conclusion is narrower: it is not reliable evidence of a functioning engine or precise mechanics parameters. Do not claim it is captured gameplay, name its engine, or derive exact damage formulas.

## Interpretation, kept separate from observation

- **Supported visual intent:** close, readable melee; an enemy weapon raised before a heavy strike; player sword follow-through; brief localized spark/red impact accents; enemy torso reactions; a heavier finishing presentation followed by a grounded defeated pose.
- **Plausible interpretation, not proven mechanics:** the player is counterattacking during enemy recovery; some attacks stagger the enemy; the last strike is a critical finisher. The frames can also be explained by predetermined choreography. These concepts need a deliberate game design decision before implementation.
- **Unsupported by this source:** causal button-to-action latency, a timed parry/block window, dodge i-frames, stamina cost/regeneration, cancels, four-strike queue rules, lock-on, root-motion speed, exact weapon reach, critical probability, enemy HP regeneration, player damage mitigation, XP or loot. No numerical acceptance criterion for these can honestly be taken from this clip alone.
